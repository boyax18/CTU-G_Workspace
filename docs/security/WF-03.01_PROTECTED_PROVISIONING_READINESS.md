### 3.1 Scope (OD-5)

v2.2 binds 13 defaults to Technical Super Admin. **D-102 provisions only 9 of them:**
- the 6 composition (governance) codes;
- the extension set `E`, in v2.2 default-binding index order:
  1. `access_control.school_access.inspect` (index 97);
  2. `access_control.role_assignments.ordinary.manage` (index 98);
  3. `access_control.audit.school.read` (index 102).

It does **not** provision these four, which are intentional scope boundaries:

| Excluded default | Why | Separate adoption decision needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `access_control.diagnostics.read` (GOV) | Not in the approved extension; bound to Technical Super Admin only | Its own owner decision and review before any provisioning |
| `accounts.profile.self.read`, `accounts.profile.self.update` (OWN) | Bound to every Workspace role, so not a governance code; holders get them through Instructor/Admin once those permissions are adopted | Adoption with the self-profile workflow; none of the 4 `ctug_workspace` roles has them today |
| `accounts.authentication_events.governance.read` (EVENT_GOV) | WF-12 event-source code; dormant | WF-12.01/12.02 approval of the event source and privacy contract |

`E` is a reviewed constant. At run time it must equal exactly:
> (Technical Super Admin's `access_control.*` defaults in v2.2) − (the 6 composition codes) − {`access_control.diagnostics.read`}

Each code of `E` must be on `access_control`, bound only to Technical Super Admin, with readiness P. Any
difference fails closed. No new permission code, role or privilege is introduced.

### 3.2 Accepted governance states (OD-1)

| State | Permission definitions and bindings | Receipts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **S0** | the 6 composition codes, each with exactly one active binding on the active Technical Super Admin role and nothing else on that role | 1 `framework.role.create` + 6 `framework.permission.create` + 6 `framework.role_binding.create` |
| **S1** | S0 plus all three codes of `E`, each with exactly one active binding on that role (9 definitions, 9 bindings) | 1 + 9 + 9 |

**Every other state is refused**, including:
- 1 or 2 codes of `E`;
- `diagnostics.read` or any other code on the role;
- `E` without the full base;
- `E` bound to Admin or any other role.

Counts alone never establish validity. Every accepted state also needs, field by field:
- definitions equal to v2.2 (service, name, module, action, description);
- the role's description;
- active bindings on that role only;
- receipt identities, order and operator provenance (section 3.4).

### 3.3 Provisioning operations

- **Amended by D-107 (19 November 2026, local development):** until D-102 is implemented, the first grant creates **S0** with the existing code: the six v2.0 governance codes, field-identical to v2.2, receipts 1 + 6 + 6. S1 is reached only through `extend-technical-governance` (OD-2), once OD-7 is met. The next bullet is the D-102 design for a first grant made after D-102 is implemented; whether that future first grant creates S1 directly is confirmed with the D-102 implementation authorization. An existing S0 must always be accepted (OD-1).
- **First grant** (`grant-technical-super-admin` when no governance profile exists): creates S1 in one
  transaction. The receipt order is role, the 6 base permissions, the 6 base bindings, then the 3
  extension permissions and the 3 extension bindings, all from the one operator.
- **Grant on an existing S0 or S1:** verifies the state and creates nothing. A grant never extends S0
  to S1 (OD-2).
- **`extend-technical-governance`** (OD-2): a new protected operation with its own exact confirmation.
  - It accepts only a fully verified S0. It refuses S1 as already extended, and refuses every other state.
  - It creates the 3 extension permissions, their 3 bindings and their 6 receipts atomically.
  - It assigns no role and runs only through the protected Django-Superuser procedure.
- **Partial base profiles (OD-6):** neither operation repairs a partial or inconsistent base. That covers
  the role without all 6 codes, codes without the role, missing or inactive bindings, and missing or
  extra receipts. Today's `_ensure_governance` can complete missing base codes when the role exists; that
  behaviour is removed. Such a state is refused for investigation and an independently authorized
  recovery (D-99).
- **Single source:** all definitions come only from `validation_v2_2.validate_catalogue()`, which validates
  v2.0 and v2.1 first.
- **All-or-nothing:** everything runs inside `configure_role`'s `writer_atomic` (READ COMMITTED). Any
  failure, including an audit failure, rolls back every row and receipt of the operation.
- **Unchanged:** the protected-writer refusal and the disposable-database-only target (D-93/D-95 still
  required for `ctug_workspace`).

### 3.4 Receipt contract (OD-3)

- **Permission receipt:** actor = the block's operator; role, target and `old_value` are None; reason
  `permission_change`; `new_value = {permission_id, service_id}`.
- **Binding receipt:** `role_id` = the Technical Super Admin role; target and `old_value` are None;
  `new_value = {role_id, permission_id, role_permission_id, is_active: True}`. The binding's `granted_by`
  must equal the block's operator.
- **Two blocks:**
  - **Base block:** the role receipt, then the 6 base permission receipts, then the 6 base binding
    receipts, all after the registration P4 release.
  - **Extension block:** the 3 extension permission receipts, then the 3 extension binding receipts, all
    after the last base receipt.
- **One operator per block:** each block has exactly one actor, who must be a staff + superuser account
  holding no Workspace role and designated at the time of its operation.
- **Different operators allowed:** the extension operator may differ from the base operator (OD-3). Each
  identity is preserved in its own receipts, and the chronological order is enforced by receipt ID.
- **Refused:** a missing, duplicated, forged, reordered, stale or mismatched receipt; a receipt for a foreign
  or inactive row; anonymous or non-framework actors.
- **Deny-preservation receipts (C-1, extension only):** after its 3 binding receipts, the extension block
  holds one `framework.governance.deny_preserved` receipt per affected holder (section 3.5.3), and none when
  no holder is affected. These receipts are outside the OD-1 counts (1 + 9 + 9), which cover only the role,
  permission and binding actions. They are still verified, never ignored: same operator and block, after
  the extension bindings, field-exact. A first-grant S1 has none.

### 3.5 Deny and authority (OD-4)

- **Grant post-check:** after a grant, the target must have `has_technical_authority` for all 9 provisioned
  codes in S1 (the 6 in S0). A Deny on any of them for **that target account** refuses the grant and rolls
  everything back.
- **Other accounts' Deny overrides** don't affect a grant. P3B, P3D and P4 keep refusing any account-level
  override inside their exercise scope.
- **Effective-permission checks:** `has_permission` and `has_technical_authority` are unchanged. Deny wins,
  and inactive account, role or service denies. School Admin never receives `E`. Technical Super Admin
  keeps School Admin operations through its Admin membership and can't grant or revoke Technical Super
  Admin; only the protected Django-Superuser procedure does.
#### 3.5.1 C-1: Deny preservation during an extension (OWNER APPROVED, 19 November 2026)

An authorized S0-to-S1 `extend-technical-governance` operation **may proceed** even if an existing
Technical Super Admin holder has an account-specific Deny on one or more extension permissions, provided
that:
1. every existing Deny stays unchanged;
2. every Deny keeps precedence over Grants and role-based permissions;
3. the extension never removes, modifies or overrides a Deny;
4. affected Technical Super Admin holders are identified and reported to the authorized operator;
5. audit evidence records the extension outcome and the affected holders;
6. the system never reports a holder as having an effective permission that a Deny blocks;
7. the extension never becomes a way to bypass access-control restrictions.

**Scope:** C-1 applies only to extending an existing valid S0 profile. It does **not** relax OD-4: a new
Technical Super Admin grant is still refused when the target account has a Deny on any required (provisioned)
permission. The extension never removes a Deny and never raises the denied account's effective authority.

#### 3.5.2 Three distinct layers

| Layer | What the extension does | Where it lives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Global role-permission extension | Adds the 3 extension permissions and 3 active bindings on the Technical Super Admin role, which applies to every holder of the role | `permissions`, `role_permissions` (plus receipts) |
| Account-specific Deny | **Nothing.** The extension issues no INSERT, UPDATE or DELETE on account overrides; every Deny row is byte-identical before and after | `account_permissions` (`effect = Deny`) |
| Effective permission per holder | Derived, never stored: `has_technical_authority` = active approved account + active Admin + Technical Super Admin roles + `has_permission`, with Deny winning and service state checked. A holder with a Deny on an extension code doesn't gain that code. | `services.has_permission` and `technical_admin.has_technical_authority` (unchanged) |

#### 3.5.3 Reporting and audit evidence (design for the implementation)

- **Affected holder:** an account that holds the Technical Super Admin role at the time of the extension and
  has an active Deny on at least one extension permission.
- **Computation:** inside the extension transaction, after the 3 bindings are written, the procedure computes
  each holder's effective status for the 3 codes with the unchanged `has_technical_authority`. It never uses
  binding presence alone, so a Deny-blocked code is never reported as effective (condition 6).
- **Audit evidence:** one `framework.governance.deny_preserved` receipt per affected holder, written in the
  same transaction and fitting the existing `audit.py` allowlist without any change to the audit contract:
  - actor = the extension operator;
  - target = the holder;
  - `role_id` = the Technical Super Admin role; `permission` = None;
  - `old_value` = None;
  - `new_value = {"account_id": <holder>, "permission_ids": [<denied extension permission IDs>], "effect": "Deny"}`;
  - reason `access_review`.

  The extension outcome itself is proven by the 3 extension permission receipts and the 3 binding receipts.
  An audit failure rolls the whole extension back (condition 5).
- **Operator report:** shown only after a successful commit, never before. It goes only to the authenticated,
  designated operator in the TTY session of the protected command.
  - It states the extension outcome, the number of affected holders and, for each, the internal account ID
    and the denied extension permission codes.
  - It contains no names, School IDs, emails or other personal data.
  - It is not written to application logs, which keep only exception type names.
  - It is not exposed through any API or screen; any future view needs WF-12 approval and
    `has_technical_authority`.
- **Wording:** the report and any later view must describe effective authority, for example "extended;
  2 holders keep a Deny on 1 extension code". They must never say that all holders now hold the codes.
- **No bypass (condition 7):** the extension is available only to the designated Django-Superuser operator,
  with re-authentication and an exact confirmation. It reads Denies only to report them and never changes
  them. Removing a Deny remains a separate, authorized account-permission change.
- **P3B/P3D/P4 interaction:** their `_snapshot` already refuses any account-level override within the
  exercise scope. A database that contains a Deny therefore can't run those commands until the override is
  resolved under its own authorization, and C-1 doesn't change that.
- **Design detail for confirmation at implementation authorization:** the receipt action name and reason
  above are proposals that fit the existing contract. P3B must verify these receipts structurally (operator,
  block position, target held the role, `permission_ids` ⊆ the extension permission IDs, `effect` Deny).
  It can't require the historical Deny to still exist, because an authorized later change may remove it.

### 3.7 Known risks

- **R-1, lock-order inversion: CORRECTED for K-1, K-2, K-3 and K-5 (19 November 2026, owner-authorized Option A
  and the K-5 correction).**
  - **Cause:**
    - `p3b._snapshot` (used by P3B, P3D and P4) locked the Admin role, the actor's account, every Admin
      assignment and the account overrides before the service rows.
    - `role_management.lock_state` locks the service rows first. The Django-Superuser procedure
      (`framework_roles.configure_role`) also locked the operator's account (`_authenticate_operator`) before the
      service rows.
    - The resulting cycles were K-1 (Admin role row), K-2 (actor account), K-3 (Admin assignments) and K-5 (the
      operator's account). K-5 comes from the snapshot's Admin-eligibility join (`account_roles` JOIN `accounts`
      JOIN `roles` `FOR UPDATE`), which, depending on the MySQL plan, can lock account rows outside its result.
    - All of these were reproduced as MySQL 1213 deadlocks on disposable databases. InnoDB's deadlock report
      identified the K-5 rows.
  - **Correction (lock order only; no policy, scope or write change):**
    - `_snapshot` first locks all service rows by `service_id`, then continues unchanged (Admin role, actor,
      Admin assignments, overrides, services re-read, permissions, bindings, receipts).
    - `configure_role` first locks the two service rows (`access_control`, `accounts`) by `service_id`, inside
      its existing `writer_atomic` transaction, then authenticates the operator and continues unchanged
      (`lock_state`, the Admin role, the governance rows).
    - The Technical Super Admin assignment path already started with `lock_state`.
    - Every protected transaction above now takes the shared service rows first, so these operations queue on
      them instead of waiting on each other.
  - **Authentication unchanged:** taking the lock never authenticates or authorizes anyone. A wrong password is
    still refused with nothing written. Credential prompts stay outside the transaction. The service rows are
    now also held during one password verification (about 0.38 s with PBKDF2-SHA256 at 1,500,000 iterations on
    the development machine).
  - **Evidence:** focused test_wf0301_r1_lock_order 10/10 (three consecutive runs, 0 deadlock or lock-wait errors); affected regression of every apps.access_control and apps.accounts module (83 modules) 1899/1899, 0 failed, 0 errors, 0 skipped; manage.py check no issues; makemigrations --check no changes. The K-5 fixture (actor, two Technical Super Admin holders, a student and the
    framework operator) is preserved because the cycle depends on the join plan.
  - **Not claimed:** that every possible deadlock is eliminated. Only K-1, K-2, K-3 and K-5 are covered by
    this part.
  - **K-4 and K-6: CORRECTED for the tested scenarios (19 November 2026, owner-authorized Option A, recorded
    under R-1).**
    - **Cause:** the P1 (`_apply_phase_one`), P2 (`_apply_phase_two`) and WF-02.05 (`run`) initializers locked
      the Admin role and the actor's account before the service rows. The initializers hold the advisory lock
      `ctug_review_p1_metadata_v1`, but the role procedures and the read-only P3D/P4 compensation previews do
      not take it. The resulting cycles:
      - K-4a: a P2 rerun or WF-02.05 (including `verify`) vs a Superuser role change;
      - K-4b: a P1 rerun vs a role change on the P1 actor;
      - K-6: P2 or WF-02.05 `verify` vs the P3D or P4 preview. The R-1 snapshot change created this one, and
        it was reachable on `ctug_workspace`.
    - **Pre-fix evidence:** every scenario was reproduced as a MySQL 1213 deadlock. The P4 preview and WF-02.05
      wrap that error as an ordinary refusal, so the tests check the whole exception chain.
    - **Correction:** each of the three initializers first locks all service rows by `service_id`, inside its
      existing durable transaction. Everything else is unchanged: the advisory lock, the Admin role, the actor,
      refusals, results, receipts and the no-repair rule.
    - **Fresh database:** with an empty `services` table the P1 prelude locks nothing (READ COMMITTED takes no
      gap locks), so it gives **no** mutual exclusion there. The tests verify that this is safe:
      - a Superuser grant refuses immediately (no services) and never waits;
      - once P1 has inserted an uncommitted service row, a grant and the P3D preview wait **while holding
        nothing**, then refuse because the services are inactive;
      - P2 and WF-02.05 are refused at once by the advisory lock;
      - a full bootstrap produces the same records and receipts, and reruns write nothing.
    - **Evidence:** test_wf0301_k4_initializer_lock_order 11/11 together with test_wf0301_r1_lock_order 10/10 in three consecutive runs, 0 MySQL 1213 or 1205 errors; affected regression of every apps.access_control and apps.accounts module (84 modules) 1910/1910, 0 failed, 0 errors, 0 skipped; manage.py check no issues; makemigrations --check no changes.
    - **Not claimed:** that every possible deadlock is eliminated. Only K-1, K-2, K-3, K-4a, K-4b, K-5 and K-6, as
      tested, are covered.
    - The operating rule stays in force as defence in depth until the owner decides otherwise: do not rerun the legacy initializers concurrently with Technical Super Admin role operations, P3D or P4.

      lower values (SQLSTATE 45000) for every user, including the restricted runtime account. Neither the runtime

      (asserted).
    - **SR-1a (confirmed, NOT changed):**
      - The public CSRF endpoint never runs `get_user`, yet `SessionTimeoutMiddleware` records activity, so a

      - After Stage A that row cannot authenticate. It matters only if the counter is also rolled back (a DBA or a
        restore).

        session hash.
    - **Residual risks:**
      - a database administrator, or a full restore that also restores the counter;
      - same-key re-creation with a copied password hash and counter (DBA-level);
      - requests already authorized before a revocation commits finish under their earlier snapshot.

      revocations recorded since the backup from the external record. Checkpoints should later cover

  - **Least privilege (tested on disposable databases only; not applied to `ctug_workspace`):**
    - **Runtime account:** `SELECT, INSERT, LOCK TABLES` on the schema, plus `UPDATE, DELETE` on every table
      **except** `access_audit_logs`.
      - It runs the real P1, P2, P4, WF-02.05, Superuser-grant, P3D-preview and D-85 paths.
      - It **cannot** update, delete, truncate, drop or alter receipts, or create triggers, tables or users, or
        grant privileges.
      - `LOCK TABLES` is required because the existing `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` reads on receipts need it; `UPDATE`
        is not.
      - It can still **insert** a forged receipt. Only the protected executor's signed requests (D-90) would stop
        that.
    - **Migration account:** `CREATE, ALTER, DROP, INDEX, REFERENCES, SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE` on its

  - **Checkpoint prototype** (`backend/apps/access_control/audit_checkpoint.py`, command
    `report_access_audit_checkpoint`; read-only, not wired into any governance or session path):
    - It is a SHA-256 hash chain over every persisted receipt field in receipt-ID order, verified against a
      checkpoint **held outside the database**, optionally HMAC-authenticated with a key the database cannot
      read.
    - It detects modified fields, timestamps or actors; deleted middle or newest anchored rows; renumbered IDs;
      forged or duplicate rows inside the anchored range; a tampered restore; malformed or wrongly keyed
      checkpoints.
    - It **does not** verify rows committed after the checkpoint (it reports them as unanchored).
    - It **fails closed** if a lower receipt ID was still uncommitted when the checkpoint was taken, so
      checkpoints need a quiescent window.
